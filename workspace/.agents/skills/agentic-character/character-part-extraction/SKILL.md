## Front A-pose

Inspect the supplied image. Use GPT ImageGen to generate a clean, straight-on, full-body A-pose from the original reference. In Pi Studio call studio_imagegen with stage=apose and the original asset ID. Preserve identity, body proportions, hair silhouette, colors, costume layers and footwear. Keep hands away from the torso and both feet on the ground line. Place independent handheld props beside the character with clear space. Save and inspect the full-resolution A-pose for clipping and identity drift.

## Astra 2D extraction

After the ImageGen A-pose passes review, use that exact output as the reference for GPT-6 Astra to plan and review the 2D extraction, and use GPT ImageGen to produce the part sheets and corrections. In Pi Studio call studio_imagegen with stage=split and the accepted ImageGen outputId/image1.png. Use Tripo for downstream mesh, UV and texture tasks. In Pi Studio, if Astra with ImageGen is unavailable, save the A-pose checkpoint and ask for the reviewed Astra part PNGs to be uploaded to the same task. Continue from those parts after confirming they match the saved A-pose.

1. Decide part ownership before generating. Include the full neck and natural human ears in the bald `head-neck` image. End the headless `body` at the base-of-neck seam and match skin and proportions to the A-pose. If coverage is needed, use plain opaque technical underlayers with a visibly different shape or finish from the extracted costume. Keep mutually occluding costume layers together when they define one silhouette. Preserve continuous dresses and keep sleeves, lapels and trim with their garment. Assign each worn detail to one owner and isolate standalone props.
   - `hair` owns animal ears, including cat ears, and ear-shaped hair accessories. Draw their bases connected to the hair. Reconstruct the hair hidden by the face, neck, clothing or ornaments, including the inner locks and back volume that should show once those parts are removed. Preserve the visible silhouette, color and strand flow. Fill any head-shaped or neck-shaped cutouts caused by removing the occluder and retain genuine gaps between free strands.
   - `head-neck` owns the fitted choker or neckband. Include its visible band, trim and any pendant attached to that band as part of the same image.
   - `body` owns a separate necklace that hangs over the chest. Keep its chain and pendant together at the A-pose placement. A choker and a chest necklace on the same character belong to their respective owners and stay separate from each other.
   - Give removable socks and stockings their own `socks` or `stockings` part. A matching left/right pair can share one PNG. Keep the cuff, stretch contours and original openings in the legwear image. Show the open areas as negative space without skin or shoe geometry. In `body`, reconstruct the uncovered legs, ankles, feet and toes with continuous skin tone and the same anatomy and pose.
2. Ask Astra for a front-view atlas with one complete asset per cell. Export each owned detail only within its owner's PNG. Put the applicable ownership, attachment, exclusions and hair completion rules in each affected part's own generation instructions so they also apply to individual redraws. Use this ownership map when interpreting generic tool prompts. Specify the ordered semantic names, grid, background and gutters. A 3 × 2 layout works for five or six parts, with an empty last cell for five. Use a different grid or multiple sheets for larger sets. Preserve the accepted A-pose for scale and placement.
3. Inspect the actual cell boundaries before slicing the atlas into independently named PNGs. Preserve the original atlas. Compare hair, head-neck, body and legwear against the accepted A-pose. Check for occlusion-shaped holes, incomplete inner hair volume, detached ear bases and missing, duplicated or misplaced accessories. Check that the body has complete legs and feet, that the legwear shows its original openings, and that neither image carries the other's surface details. Also check body proportions, garment ownership, color and clipping. When fixing a defect, redraw the affected part while preserving the proportions and placement from the same A-pose. If the full reference keeps reintroducing the face-shaped opening, edit the isolated hair image and keep its existing fringe, outline and ears while filling the opening with inner hair. Record inferred hidden details.
4. Save the A-pose, atlas and one full-resolution PNG per part. Record source and A-pose hashes, the generation tool, ordered part names, file paths, ownership and review status. Show the A-pose and parts before advancing when the user requests a review. Pass the selected individual PNG for each part to component-gen.
